Core Idea

Conventional recurrent neural networks compress sequence history into a fixed-dimensional vector, limiting expressiveness and test-time adaptability. WARP (Weight-space Adaptive Recurrent Prediction) instead parametrises the recurrent hidden state directly as the weights and biases of an auxiliary neural network, updated linearly from input differences.

Key Takeaways

01

Gradient-Free Adaptation

Updating auxiliary network parameters through a linear recurrence enables fast, gradient-free test-time adaptation and in-context learning directly in weight space.

02

Physics-Informed Priors

Because the hidden state is an explicit function approximator, domain-specific physical priors plug in naturally—yielding a physics-informed variant that outperforms the next best baseline by >10×.

03

Broad Sequence Generalisation

Ranks in the top three across 5 of 6 challenging real-world classification datasets, and excels on sequential image completion, multivariate time series forecasting, and dynamical system reconstruction.
